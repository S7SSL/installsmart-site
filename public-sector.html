Case Study: Measuring AI Crawler Access on a UK Publisher

Which AI crawlers read a site, whether they obey the rules set for them and how the content then appears in AI answers

For a UK online legal-services publisher with more than 100 original guidance pages, we identified every crawler visiting the site from server and edge logs, set access rules per AI user agent in robots.txt and at network level, published machine-readable structured data and then measured how AI assistants read and cited the content.

420k
requests analysed over 30 days (Cloudflare, sampled)
21
declared AI crawlers and agents identified, about 7% of traffic
17%
of traffic from automated clients that did not identify themselves

Outcome: AI discoverability score raised from 89 to 100/100 and the site became the most-cited domain in its category across AI assistants. More usefully for policy, the exercise produced a repeatable method for separating declared from undeclared crawlers and for testing whether published preferences are honoured.

  • Log analysis that separates declared AI crawlers from undeclared automated clients
  • Per-agent access rules tested against observed behaviour over time
  • Visitor identity checked against each operator's published IP ranges and verification methods
